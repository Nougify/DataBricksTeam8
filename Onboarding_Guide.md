# **Data Intelligence for Smarter Communities Hackathon** Student Onboarding Guide 

**Welcome!** This guide will help you prepare for the Rogers and Databricks Data Intelligence for Smarter Communities Hackathon at UBC. 

## **The Challenge** 

Students must create a smart community solution that focuses on **Transit** or/and **Security** based off cell tower traffic data. Students will have access to Databricks and a table of synthetic cell tower traffic data across points of interest in Vancouver. The goal is to uncover actionable insights, build a solution around those insights, and demonstrate the solution via a prototype, dashboard, application, notebook, or presentation. 

## **Creating Your Databricks Account** 

Students will be using Databricks to analyze the provided data. Sign up <u>here</u> for a free Databricks account. 

Students may want to get familiar with Databricks prior to the hackathon. Here is the Databricks <u>learning portal. Some areas to explore are: Lakehouse Fundamentals, Tutorials, and Solution Accelerators.</u> 

You may utilize outside data sources in addition to the synthetic cell tower traffic data provided, if they are open source. 

## **Tips For Success** 

Start with understanding the data, focus on one clear problem, develop a compelling story, create an easy-to-use visual experience, and ensure recommendations are supported by evidence. 

For team setup, we recommend that one team member create a Databricks Free Edition workspace and invite the rest of their team members, granting them admin permissions as needed. Once the workspace is configured, a designated team member can download the hackathon files, notebooks, and datasets and upload them to the shared workspace so the entire team can access them. When collaborating in shared notebooks, please avoid using the "Run All" feature, as it may lead to runtime conflicts when multiple team members are working in the same environment simultaneously.

## **Judging Format** 

### **Phase 1: Round Robin** 

Teams will be split across two rooms. Judges will also be split across the rooms. Each team will present once during the round robin (5-minute presentations). The top two scoring teams from each room will advance to the final round, for a total of four finalist teams. 

### **Phase 2: Final Presentations** 

The four finalist teams will present again to all judges and the broader group (5-minute presentations). Final scores will determine first, second, and third place. 

## **Presentation Timing** 

Each judging slot is expected to include **a 5-minute team presentation** , 3 minutes for Q&A, and 2 minutes for team transition and final scoring. Teams should keep their presentation focused and practice in advance so they can clearly explain their problem, insight, solution, and impact within the allotted time.

## **How Scores Are Calculated** 

Judges will complete scorecards for each team they evaluate. Scores will be collected and tallied by the event team. In the round robin phase, the top two scoring teams from each room will advance to the finals. In the final round, judges will score the finalist teams to determine first, second, and third place. 

If there is a tie, the judges will review the top-scoring teams and collectively rank them to determine final placement.
